    Topics from around the world

    A phrase is listed under a country when that country's trade press accounts for far more of its coverage than that country's share of the whole corpus would predict. The comparison is against the corpus, not raw counts — otherwise every phrase would look American simply because most feeds are. Country means where a publication is based, not what an article is about: a US paper writing about China is US coverage. Nothing here says one country used a phrase before another. A phrase can appear under more than one country — two small corpora can both carry it more than their size predicts, which is a real finding rather than a fault. Those are marked shared.

    Regulators and agencies named in coverage

    Institutions are cross-sector by nature — a regulator does not belong to one sector — so several sectors naming the same body is a different kind of observation from a phrase spreading. Names are read from article summaries, where capitalisation means something; headlines are often title case and would make every phrase look like a name. Articles without a summary contribute nothing, so this undercounts rather than guesses.

    Finer details within a topic

    A broad phrase says little on its own — "data center" could be a property story, a power story or a chip story. What sits beside it is what makes it specific. A companion is listed when it appears alongside this topic far more often than its share of coverage would predict — not merely because it is common. Ranking by raw co-occurrence surfaced the topic's own vocabulary restated, which said nothing. Companions that share a word with the phrase are excluded for the same reason. Two separate readings. Being used more compares a phrase against its own previous 14 days, so the evidence is on both sides of the comparison. First appearance has no previous 14 days to compare with, which makes it thinner — and adding a publication to the reading list produces a burst of them that reflects our coverage changing rather than the conversation changing. They are kept apart so neither displaces the other. Both describe what is being discussed, not what happens next.
